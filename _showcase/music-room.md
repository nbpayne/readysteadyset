The brief was simple: create a space that felt like it had been lived in by a musician for twenty years, not built last Tuesday.

We used eight flats to build a three-sided room in a Marrickville warehouse studio. The configuration gave us a rear wall, two side walls with a corner between them, and a dedicated window flat on the left-hand side - facing away from the studio's existing skylights so we could control exactly how the "natural" light read on camera.

## The configuration

The room measured roughly 4.8m wide by 3.6m deep - a tight but believable space. The corner window was positioned to camera left, with practicals rigged to the outside of the flat to simulate afternoon sun streaming in. On camera, it read perfectly.

The art department dressed the walls with salvaged acoustic foam tiles, faded band posters, a framed setlist from a 1994 show, and a clock that didn't move. The floor was covered with a worn Persian rug.

## Lighting advantages

Because we were in a studio, the gaffer could position a Skypanel above the window opening and dial it from a warm gold afternoon to a flat overcast grey within seconds - without moving a single fixture. We shot the "golden hour" version and the "flat day" version within the same morning.

That would have been impossible on location.

## Day two reconfiguration

On the second day, we removed one flat from the left-hand wall and repositioned it to create a corridor entrance - giving the director a new angle and a fresh eyeline. The move took about forty minutes. We gained a completely different-feeling location without leaving the studio.
